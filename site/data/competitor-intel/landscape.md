### 1. Pocket Prep — threat: **HIGH** (holds first)

**Their pitch:** the eight-minute study session. 22,000+ questions across 21 certifications, 2,450 on PMP, native iOS and Android, in market since 2011.

**What changed:** they are demonstrably current on the July 2026 PMP outline and say so on the exam page; they run a real editorial operation under a named PMP-credentialed Director of Education; and their annual plan is **$124.99/yr, $10.99/mo effective — cheaper per month than CipherExam Pro.** Their pass guarantee is three extra months of access, not money back.

**Our gap to exploit:** their feedback resolves to a domain and never to a person. Explanations authored once for everyone, analytics that report subject scores and a peer percentile. Nothing in the product holds a fact about one candidate's reasoning. This is architectural, and more content does not close it.

**Positioning angle:** diagnosis, not authorship, and never price. See the deep dive.

### 2. ExamCert — threat: **HIGH — new entrant, straight in at second**

**Their pitch:** free apps, then **$4.99–$5.99 one-time per exam for lifetime access with free updates forever.** 171+ certifications claimed, 13,800 free questions, founded 2025. Written explanations on every option rather than only the correct one, an AI Tutor that "walks you through the reasoning step by step," FSRS spaced repetition, a full timed simulator, a **per-domain mastery heatmap** and an estimated pass chance. Content "curated by certified professionals," updated every two weeks. And a **60-day no-questions-asked money-back guarantee.**

**Why they rank this high on entry:** read that feature list against ours. Step-by-step reasoning, a mastery heatmap, a readiness estimate and a no-conditions 60-day refund is close to the CipherExam pitch, at roughly a thirtieth of the four-month price, in a lane where they also run a content operation publishing "best prep app 2026" comparison articles that rank for the exact queries our buyers type. They cover PMP and SHRM-CP (600+ items, free tier of 100+). Their CompTIA and Security+ coverage is not clearly established.

**What we do not know, and must not write copy against:** their own surfaces contradict each other — **95% and 98% first-attempt pass rate, $4.99 and $5.99, 30+ and 171+ certifications.** Every figure is self-reported and none is sourced. Per the standing rule on unsourced pass-rate claims, none of these numbers goes into our copy, in any direction, including as an attack. Nobody here has opened the product.

**Our gap to exploit:** unknown until someone opens it. The honest statement is that a page-level feature list is not a product, and we learned in August what it costs to write copy against a competitor we had only read about.

**Positioning angle:** hold. Verify first. Do not build a price response.

**Why they stay high on our only real cluster:** 94% of everything we reach is PMP, and on PMP they are a specialist that is already current. The stored 99.6% first-time pass claim remains unsourced and unverified — do not repeat it, and do not counter it with a number of our own.

**Our gap to exploit:** PMI-only. They cannot follow a candidate into Security+ or SHRM-CP without rebuilding the engine. No founder voice. At $299.99/yr they are the premium end of a market whose floor just fell to six dollars, which is a squeeze they will have to answer.

**Positioning angle:** breadth and the one-account argument. Never pass rates.

### The lane that is actually open

With SHRM-CP retracted, the honest answer is that **no lane in this file is uncontested.** What we have instead is an asymmetry worth naming: every competitor above competes on the content axis — how many questions, who wrote them, what they cost, how current they are. On that axis this month went badly for us in three separate places at once.

Not one of them, on any surface read this month, claims to tell a candidate **which mistake they personally keep making.** Pocket Prep reports subject scores and a peer percentile. ExamCert ships a per-domain mastery heatmap. Mindirus tailors explanations to "weak areas." All three resolve to the material. **The diagnosis axis is the open ground, and it is open because it is hard, not because nobody thought of it.**

Which puts a sharp edge on the thing this file cannot answer for itself: nobody has confirmed that a live CipherExam account produces a named trap on screen. The one defensible position left is the one whose evidence we have never checked.
