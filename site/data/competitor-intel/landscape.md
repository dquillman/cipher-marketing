## Competitive Intel: Landscape Scan

*Top five threats, ranked by likelihood of capturing the PMP / Security+ / SHRM-CP candidate*
*Generated 2026-08-29 (monthly automated refresh)*

---

> **How this month's ranking was built.** Last month ranked on structural similarity to CipherExam. That produced a defensible list but the wrong question, because a competitor that resembles us is not necessarily the one a candidate buys instead of us. This month ranks on a single test: **who is most likely to take the specific candidate we are trying to reach, on the exam we are actually reaching them on?**
>
> Two facts drive the re-ranking. First, **100% of our measured impressions are PMP** — Security+ and SHRM-CP have had zero since their landing pages shipped on 2026-05-12 — so a PMI specialist competes with us for every impression we currently buy, while a broad multi-cert platform competes for only part of one. Second, **PMI rebalanced the PMP outline on 2026-07-09** (People 42→33, Process 50→41, Business Environment 8→26), which rewards whoever has already shipped a current-outline product and quietly penalises every large legacy bank.
>
> One rule survives unchanged: **"AI-powered" is the category description now**, said by at least three credible rivals on our exact certifications. It cannot carry a headline.

### 1. Pocket Prep — threat: **HIGH**

**Their pitch:** Mobile-first drilling that fits the gaps of a working day, with expert-written explanations and textbook references on every item. 22,000+ questions across 21 certifications, covering all three of our priority exams. Adaptive practice weighted toward weak domains. Fifteen years in market and the app-store trust that comes with it. $14.99–$29.99/month, or roughly $9.99–$20 for one exam bank.

**Our gap to exploit:** Feedback resolves to the *domain*, never to the *person* — explanations are authored once for everyone, and adaptivity says "you are weak in Risk" and stops. No per-user reasoning-pattern tracking and no cognitive-level classification underneath. The single-MCQ format cannot rehearse CompTIA performance-based questions at all. And their greatest asset is now a liability on PMP specifically: ~2,450 items built against an outline that changed in July.

**Positioning angle:** Diagnosis versus review, and currency versus size. *"A big question bank is a photograph of the exam on the day it was written."* On Security+, lead with native PBQ support instead — that is an engine gap volume cannot close.

**Why still first:** they are the default search result and the default forum recommendation on all three of our exams. Every other name in this file is a specialist by comparison. Full analysis in this month's deep dive.

---

### 2. Brain Sensei — threat: **MEDIUM-HIGH overall, HIGH on PMP alone — moved up from third**

**Their pitch:** The strongest published pass-rate claim in PMI prep (99.6% first-time pass), the full eight-certification PMI portfolio under consistent UX, adaptive learning with spaced repetition, a private community, a pass guarantee, story-based content rather than passive video, **auto-updated for the 2026 PMP refresh**, and a free seven-day trial. $349.99/year on sale (regular $499.99), or $99/month.

**Our gap to exploit:** The 99.6% figure is unverifiable — no methodology is disclosed anywhere. Their pass guarantee is conditioned (thirty-day window, limited exams). They are PMI-only and cannot follow a candidate into Security+ or SHRM-CP without rebuilding the engine. There is no named founder or instructor — a corporate brand with nobody behind it. And they are roughly five times our monthly price.

**Positioning angle:** Do not attack the number; attack what kind of number it is. *"A pass rate is a statistic about other people. The useful question is what a tool does to the way you read a question."* Then let the structural facts speak: $19/month against $99/month, twelve live certifications against eight PMI ones, and a founder with a real story page. The PMI AI Standards Core Team endorsement is a human trust signal they have no equivalent for — on PMI surfaces it runs with the institutional credential and the name removed.

**Why the move to second:** the ranking question is who takes *our* candidate. Ours is currently a PMP candidate, because that is the only cluster with impressions. Brain Sensei is the only competitor in this file that is simultaneously PMI-specialist, already shipped on the 2026 outline, and polished enough to win a head-to-head on brand. Their ceiling — structural entrapment inside PMI — is real but it does not protect us on the one exam where we are actually competing today.

### 3. Mindirus — threat: **HIGH (unverified) — moved down from second**

**Their pitch:** AI coaching plus adaptive spaced repetition across 16 certifications on a bank reported at 51,000–54,000 questions, with AI-generated explanations, study plans and progress tracking, starting from a free tier. Covers CISSP and AWS — second and third in our own exam priority order, both still marked coming soon on our side.

**Our gap to exploit:** **Still unknown, one full month later.** Nobody here has signed up or looked at a single question. We do not know whether their AI explanation is metacognitive or merely fluent, whether anything is classified by cognitive level, or whether "adaptive" means more than spaced repetition on missed items. Their bank-size claim is self-reported and inconsistent across their own surfaces (51,000 versus 54,000).

**Positioning angle:** **None, deliberately, for the second month running.** We spent August unwinding copy built on an assumption about a competitor's explanations. Repeating that against a product nobody here has opened would be the same error with a different logo.

**Why the move to third:** nothing about them got weaker — the ranking criterion changed. On structural similarity they are still the closest analogue to CipherExam ever recorded in this file. On *likelihood of taking our current candidate*, a multi-cert platform whose flagship coverage is CISSP and AWS is competing hardest for candidates we are not yet reaching. That reasoning inverts the moment we ship CISSP. **Their rank is an estimate standing in for a fact, and it will keep being one until somebody opens the product** — which is why it is the first recommended action for the second consecutive month.

### 4. The AI-tutor app cohort — threat: **HIGH (message) / MEDIUM (product)**

*FlashGenius, "PMP Exam Prep 2026: AI Tutor", and the rest of the cohort.*

**Their pitch:** AI flashcards, exam simulations, personalised learning paths, named AI mentor characters. One markets a study mentor that analyses performance and directs study, claiming 2,000+ PMP questions updated for the 2026 format and top-app positioning in AI-powered PMP practice.

**Our gap to exploit:** Individually thinner than us — flashcard and simulation layers behind a conversational wrapper, mostly single-cert or shallow multi-cert, with no evidence of a classification pipeline or per-user pattern tracking underneath. A named AI mentor character is a UI choice, not a pedagogy.

**Positioning angle:** Fight the phrase, not the products. Move the lead claim off "AI-powered" and onto mechanisms a prospect can check on a screen: Bloom's cognitive-level classification, per-user thinking-traps tracking, native PBQ support on the CompTIA family, and the Exam Lens as a per-exam framework tied to the standard the exam tests against.

**Why fourth:** the damage they do is linguistic rather than commercial. Collectively they are commoditising our headline faster than any one of them could take a customer, and that damage is permanent — the phrase is not coming back. Worth noting that several of them advertise 2026-format PMP updates, which makes speed-to-current-outline a contested field rather than an open one.

### 5. Examzify — threat: **MEDIUM**

**Their pitch:** Free, clean UI, flashcards and MCQ with hints and per-item explanations, an enormous long tail of exams reaching well beyond IT, and a subdomain per exam that captures long-tail search.

**Our gap to exploit:** Free-tier economics produce thin, uneven, inconsistently maintained banks. No progress model, no diagnosis, no accountability. A mile wide and an inch deep — not a serious preparation path for any of our three priority exams.

**Positioning angle:** Compete on the search lane, not on price, and **never attack "free" directly** — our own free tier is the top of our funnel and an attack on free is an attack on it. Attack what happens after the click: *"Free practice questions are everywhere. The question is whether anything is learning about you while you answer them."* Our free tier is a working diagnostic — twenty questions a day for the first seven days, then five a day, permanently, with the same explanations and the same Exam Lens underneath.

**Why fifth and not lower:** they do not take our paying customer, they take the first search a nervous candidate runs — which is upstream of the paying customer and worth more than the "medium" label suggests.

Nobody in this file is competing on *reasoning* for the SHRM behavioural competencies, and the competency-map magnet already exists and serves.

It has now been identified as open in three consecutive scans with nothing shipped behind it. At that point the finding stops being intelligence and becomes a record of an unmade decision. Either a post goes to it this month or the lane should be formally deferred in writing, so the next scan does not spend a section rediscovering it.
