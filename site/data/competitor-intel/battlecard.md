## Competitive Intel: Positioning Battlecard

*CipherExam vs. the question-bank category — Pocket Prep · Examzify · MeasureUp*

Pocket Prep now ships expert-written explanations with textbook references on every question, plus adaptive practice that routes toward your weak domains. Any CipherExam copy that claims competitors don't explain answers is factually wrong and a prospect can disprove it in thirty seconds. The battlecard below is rebuilt on the gap that actually still exists.

---

### Point 1 — Explanation is table stakes. Diagnosis isn't.

**Their pitch:** Every question comes with a detailed, expert-written explanation and a textbook reference. Adaptive practice sends you back to the domains you're weakest in.

**The gap:** An explanation is written once, for everybody. It tells you why C is correct. It never tells you why *you* keep reaching for B. Their adaptivity works at domain resolution — "you're weak in Risk" — which is a syllabus fact, not a reasoning fact. Two candidates can both be weak in Risk for completely different reasons and get identical practice.

**Your angle:**
> *"Knowing your weak domain tells you what to review. It doesn't tell you what you do wrong when you read a question."*

CipherExam's thinking-traps detection tracks recurring patterns per user, per exam — the specific question shapes where that person goes wrong. That's a different resolution of feedback, not a louder version of the same one.

---

### Point 2 — A drill app can't rehearse the part of the exam that isn't multiple choice.

**Their pitch:** One question at a time, immediate feedback, huge banks, mock exams that simulate test day.

**The gap:** Security+, Network+, and A+ are not pure multiple choice. They include performance-based questions — the drag-drop, topology, and CLI-style items that candidates consistently report as the hardest part of sitting the exam. A single-MCQ drill format is structurally unable to rehearse those. This isn't a quality gap they can close with more questions; it's an engine gap.

**Your angle:**
> *"Security+ won't only ask you to pick a letter. Practice the part of it that isn't multiple choice."*

CipherExam supports PBQs natively across the CompTIA family, plus Full Mock simulators at real length and real timing — Security+ at 90 questions / 90 minutes, PMP at 180 / 230.

---

### Point 3 — Volume is a number. Cognitive level is the variable that matters.

**Their pitch:** 22,000+ questions across the catalog. 2,450 on PMP alone. 1,600 on SHRM-CP. Volume as proof of seriousness.

**The gap:** A question count says nothing about what kind of thinking the questions demand. A bank weighted toward recall builds recall. PMP and SHRM-CP are dominated by scenario judgment — apply, analyze, evaluate. A working professional who grinds 2,000 recall items has trained the wrong muscle very thoroughly, and their practice score will not warn them.

**Your angle:**
> *"You don't need more questions. You need the right kind of question, and a tool that knows the difference."*

Every CipherExam question is classified by Bloom's cognitive level before it enters the bank. That's the core IP, and it's the thing volume can't substitute for.

## Category Creation Angle

"AI-powered" is finished as a differentiator — see the landscape report. Three AI-native rivals now make the same claim on the same certs, and the phrase no longer separates us from anyone.

The word still unclaimed in this category is **diagnostic**.

**Position CipherExam as the exam reasoning trainer — the tool that finds your reasoning pattern, not just your weak domain.**

Every competitor can tell a candidate *what to study next*. None of them can tell a candidate *how they think wrong*. That sentence is the whole company.

Landing-page test line: *"Not a question bank. A diagnosis."*
