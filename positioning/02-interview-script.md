# 20-Minute Validation Call — Script

**Goal of the call:** learn whether the frame lands and the offer pulls — in *their* words. You are not selling. You are not demoing for 15 minutes. You are listening for three signals.

**Golden rule:** Talk less than 30% of the time. The moment you start explaining/defending, you've stopped learning. When they go quiet, count to three before you fill it.

**Who counts as a real prospect:** someone sitting (or seriously planning to sit) the PMP in the next ~1–3 months. NOT friends, NOT founders, NOT people who already bought a competitor and love it (those are useful but separate).

---

## The three signals you're scoring (mark each call in `05-call-log.md`)

1. **Clarity** — did they get what it is in ~10 seconds, without you re-explaining?
2. **Pull** — did they ask "how much / how do I get it?" *before* you brought up price?
3. **Objection type** — when you named the price, did they push back on **price** ("$19 is a lot") or **relevance** ("how's this different from the question bank I already have")? Price = offer problem. Relevance = frame problem. No pushback + pull = you've found the frame.

---

## SCRIPT

### 0:00 — Open (30 sec)
> "Thanks for hopping on. I'm building a PMP prep tool and I'm *not* going to pitch you — I'm trying to figure out if I'm even describing it right. Totally fine to tell me it's confusing or that you'd never use it. That's the useful answer. Cool if I ask you a few things about how you're prepping?"

### 0:30 — Their world FIRST (5 min) — *do this before you say one word about CipherExam*
Ask, then listen. Capture exact phrases.
1. "Where are you in your PMP prep right now?"
2. "What are you using to study?" *(list every tool — note if free vs paid, and what they pay)*
3. "What's the most frustrating part of prepping for this specific exam?" *(let them rant — this is gold)*
4. "Last time you took a practice question and got it wrong — what happened next? What did the tool tell you?" *(probes the explanation gap)*
5. "What's it going to cost you if you don't pass on the first try?" *(time, money, the re-sit fee, telling their boss — let them say the stakes out loud)*

> **Listen for:** do they spontaneously describe the problem CipherExam solves (no explanations, memorization not working, scenario questions feel like traps)? If they name it themselves, the frame is downstream-easy.

### 5:30 — The frame, cold (2 min)
Read ONE category frame from `01-one-pager.md` (A, B, or C — pick before the call; rotate across the 5 so you get signal on which lands). Then STOP.
> [read the frame] … "In your own words — what do you think that is?"

- **Score CLARITY now.** Did they play it back roughly right in one go? Or did they squint?
- Follow-up: "What would you expect something like that to cost per month?" *(Their number tells you what shelf they put you on. If they say "$5" you're shelved as an app. If they say "$50+" the frame worked.)*
- ⚠️ Note: you asked *them* for a price before stating yours — that's deliberate. Don't anchor them.

### 7:30 — One concrete look (3 min)
Show ONE real thing — a single PMP question with the Exam Lens explanation + the weak-spot/Readiness idea. Not a tour. One screen.
> "Here's the part that's different — when you get one wrong, it shows you the *reasoning* PMI tests for, and over time it tells you which weak spots will actually fail you. … What's your honest first reaction?"

- Listen for "oh, that's different" vs. "ok, so it's a question bank with notes."

### 10:30 — The offer + price (3 min)
> "It's $19 a month — free for 7 days, no credit card, cancel anytime. For context, re-sitting the PMP is $575 and boot camps run a few hundred."
Then STOP. Watch the reaction. Then:
- "Honest gut — too much, too little, about right?"
- **Score OBJECTION TYPE now.** Price pushback vs relevance pushback vs none.
- "Would you actually start the free trial this week? Why or why not?" *(intent — but weight it lightly; talk is cheap. The real tell was whether they asked the price unprompted earlier.)*

### 13:30 — The killer questions (4 min)
1. "What would have to be true for you to pay for this instead of [whatever they're using now]?"
2. "What would you need to *see* to believe it'll actually help you pass?" *(→ this answers our weakest Hormozi lever, Perceived Likelihood. Capture verbatim.)*
3. "If this didn't exist, what would you do instead?" *(reveals the true competitor / true alternative — might be 'nothing' or 'just YouTube')*
4. "Who else prepping for the PMP should I talk to?" *(referral → fills the next 5 calls)*

### 17:30 — Close (1 min)
> "This was exactly what I needed — thank you. Can I send you a free trial link, no strings? And if it's useful, I'd love to hear how it goes."

## Right after the call (do not skip — 3 min while it's fresh)
Fill in that call's block in `05-call-log.md`:
- The 3 scores (clarity / pull / objection type)
- Their **exact words** for: the frustration, what they'd expect to pay, what they'd need to see to believe it works
- Which frame (A/B/C) you tested
- One quote you could put on the homepage

> The verbatim quotes ARE the deliverable. "Finds the weak spots before the exam does" only works as an H1 if a real candidate says something like it back to you. Mine for the line.
